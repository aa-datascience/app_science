Write the card for this paper in today's digest. The reader sees the card first and decides from it whether to open the paper, so it must say concretely what the paper found.

You only have the title, the abstract and the figure captions. Stay within what the abstract states: do not add results, numbers or context that are not in it, and do not make the claim stronger than the authors do.

Paper: {{title}}
Authors: {{authors}}
arXiv: {{arxiv_id}} [{{categories}}]

{{abstract}}

Figures of the paper, as [label] caption:
{{figures}}

What to produce:
- plain_title: the title rewritten in plain language, as a statement of the result when the abstract allows it. No jargon the reader would not know from a physics degree.
- finding: one sentence on what they found or showed.
- why_it_matters: one sentence on why it matters for the field. If the abstract gives no reason, say what question it helps answer rather than inventing an application.
- tags: 2 to 5 short topic tags, lowercase.
- difficulty: 1 to 5, the background needed to follow the paper (1 = any physicist, 5 = specialists only).
- figure: the label of the one figure a reader would most want to see beside this card, the one that shows the main result itself. Copy the label exactly as it appears in brackets above. Prefer a result plot over a schematic, a setup photo or a supplementary figure. Use an empty string if no figure is listed or none shows the main result.

Inline math, if needed, as $...$.
